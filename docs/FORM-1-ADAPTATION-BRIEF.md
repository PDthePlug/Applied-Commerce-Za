# Form 1 Adaptation Brief

**Working source:** Grade 8 learner manuscript  
**Zimbabwe placement:** Form 1  
**Status:** Editorial conversion plan — source structure preserved until the three-term edition is rebuilt

## Form 1 purpose

Form 1 should establish the learner's relationship with identity, money, resources, work, saving, habits and community economic life before the later enterprise stages.

The Zimbabwe edition should feel recognisably local without becoming a collection of Zimbabwe references pasted onto South African stories.

## Three-term working sequence

### Term 1 — Identity, money and resources
**Source lessons:** 1–26

Core arc:
- identity and beliefs;
- personal efficacy and goals;
- meeting money;
- needs versus wants;
- saving;
- work, value and initiative;
- accurate thinking and self-discipline;
- resources, records and income.

Zimbabwe conversion work:
- replace the DBE opening with HBC competency framing;
- rebuild introductory money exercises around believable Zimbabwean household contexts;
- use USD and ZiG deliberately rather than converting every rand amount mechanically;
- introduce *mukando* as a locally familiar saving mechanism where the lesson genuinely needs a community-savings example;
- change "South Africans save..." to a Zimbabwean saving landscape including banks, mobile money, cash, savings groups and household practices.

### Term 2 — Earning, spending, budgeting and economic habits
**Source lessons:** 27–54

Core arc:
- small and creative ways to earn;
- pricing and negotiation;
- psychology of spending;
- needs versus wants revisited;
- values and spending;
- community economics;
- budgeting;
- habit formation and consistency;
- community accountability.

High-priority story conversions:
- **Emmanuel's Spaza Shop** → rebuild as **Emmanuel's Neighbourhood Shop** (or a verified Zimbabwe retail setting) while keeping Emmanuel's role, family logic, costs and profit lesson intact;
- taxi-rank scenes → kombi-rank / transport-terminus scenes where the behaviour and opportunity logic still works;
- **The Stokvel Habit** → **The Mukando Habit**;
- Umlazi savings-club narrative → a Zimbabwean *mukando* community narrative, with contribution rhythm and trust logic preserved;
- Durban / Katlehong / Soweto / Pretoria settings → distributed Zimbabwe settings chosen for narrative fit rather than one repetitive Harare substitution;
- **The Gardeners of Limpopo** → rebuild as a Zimbabwe horticulture/community-production case study after the underlying story is checked.

### Term 3 — Economic identity, project work and portfolio
**Source lessons:** 55–80

Core arc:
- becoming a saver, earner and planner;
- habit tracker project;
- reflection and review;
- capstone selection and planning;
- project execution;
- presentation;
- portfolio close;
- transition to Form 2.

Zimbabwe conversion work:
- project options should be possible for urban, peri-urban and rural learners;
- community observation tasks should recognise formal and informal economic activity without treating either as inferior;
- small-enterprise examples should include trading, services, food, agriculture and digital work where age-appropriate;
- "Looking Back at Grade 8" → "Looking Back at Form 1";
- "Looking Ahead to Grade 9" → "Looking Ahead to Form 2";
- final portfolio should explicitly connect learner evidence to HBC-relevant capabilities.

## Character continuity

Keep Myah and the recurring learner characters unless a name itself creates a story problem.

When moving a story:
1. rewrite the location and economic environment together;
2. check every callback later in the manuscript;
3. preserve family relationships and character growth;
4. do not change a lesson's moral simply to make a local reference fit.

## Money editorial rule

No automatic rand-to-dollar or rand-to-ZiG conversion.

For each money example classify it as:
- **pure arithmetic** — use simple, pedagogically clean values;
- **real-world price** — replace with a credible Zimbabwe example and verify the scale;
- **income / wage** — use a contextually credible range and date it if needed;
- **saving target** — make the target plausible for the learner's setting;
- **business cost / price** — rebuild the mini-economics of the example so profit margins remain meaningful;
- **historical story** — retain original currency only when the story is explicitly presented as a South African case study.

## Form 1 completion gate

Form 1 is ready only when:
- all 79 authored units are placed into the three-term sequence;
- DBE/South Africa-only curriculum framing is removed;
- all 275 detected rand/R examples have been reviewed in context;
- all 79 stokvel references have been reviewed and localised where appropriate;
- all spaza, taxi-rank and SA-location scenes have continuity checks;
- the final transition says Form 1 → Form 2;
- HBC competency mapping exists for every major project;
- generated curriculum, tests, typecheck, lint and production build are green.

## Terminology evidence

Zimbabwean savings literature and local financial-sector sources use **mukando** for the singular community savings arrangement and **mikando** for the plural. The term should still be explained in English the first time it appears so the material remains accessible across Zimbabwe's language communities.
