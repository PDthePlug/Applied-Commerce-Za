# Zimbabwe Localisation Standard

## Purpose

This document governs the conversion of Applied Commerce from its South African Grade 8–12 source edition into a Zimbabwe-specific secondary-school edition.

The goal is not to disguise the source material. The goal is to preserve the educational engine while making every example, institution, school transition, currency decision and learner task credible in Zimbabwe.

## 1. Structural truth before localisation

The source edition contains five authored books and four source terms per book.

The Zimbabwe core product is being structured across **Forms 1–4 only**, with three school terms per year. Forms 5–6 / A-Level are outside the scope of this edition.

The five authored source years are therefore being re-sequenced rather than relabelled.

Current source allocation:

| Zimbabwe year | Source material |
| --- | --- |
| Form 1 | Grade 8 |
| Form 2 | Corrected Grade 9 |
| Form 3 | Grade 10 + Grade 11 Terms 1–2 |
| Form 4 | Grade 11 Terms 3–4 + Grade 12 |

This creates 12 Zimbabwe delivery terms. Form 4 is the Launch Year. See `ZIMBABWE-OLEVEL-ARCHITECTURE.md` for the detailed mapping.

## 3. HBC alignment lens

Applied Commerce should be mapped to the Heritage-Based Curriculum 2024–2030 as a practical capability and enrichment programme, not represented as a prescribed ZIMSEC subject unless and until formal approval exists.

Priority competency links include:

- problem-solving;
- critical thinking;
- leadership;
- communication and teamwork;
- research;
- innovation and technological skills;
- numeracy and literacy in economic contexts;
- entrepreneurial skills;
- business and financial literacy;
- self-management;
- planning and organising;
- project-based application.

Every major unit should be able to answer four questions:

1. What does the learner understand?
2. What does the learner do?
3. What evidence is produced?
4. What HBC-relevant capability does that evidence demonstrate?

## 4. National-context conversion rules

### Education language

Convert references only when the Zimbabwe equivalent is real and contextually correct.

- DBE → HBC / Ministry of Primary and Secondary Education context as appropriate.
- Grade language → Form 1–4 or programme-stage language after final unit placement.
- matric → O-Level completion, post-O-Level transition or school completion depending on meaning.
- Grade 12 "Launch Year" concepts → Form 4 Launch Year.
- South African university/funding processes → verified Zimbabwean routes.
- NSFAS must never be replaced with a made-up "Zimbabwe NSFAS equivalent."
- A-Level must not be inserted into the core Applied Commerce pathway simply because Forms 5–6 exist.

### Money

Zimbabwe is a multi-currency environment. Currency choices must be part of the lesson rather than hidden.

- Use ZiG where a local-currency example is educationally useful.
- Use USD where the real Zimbabwean economic context commonly uses USD.
- Where a lesson is about exchange-rate risk, savings, pricing or purchasing power, the multi-currency reality can become part of the learning.
- Do not convert every historical rand amount with one exchange rate. Rebuild examples around believable local values.

### Tax, banking and institutions

- SARS → ZIMRA only when the function being taught is equivalent.
- Rebuild tax thresholds, PAYE, VAT and filing examples from current Zimbabwean rules before publication.
- Bank, mobile-money and payment examples must use current Zimbabwean products or generic language when provider facts may date quickly.
- Legal-adulthood, contracts, consumer protection and employment examples require Zimbabwe-specific legal review before final publication.

### Community economic life

Localisation should feel lived-in rather than cosmetic.

Candidate equivalents include:

- stokvel → mukando / savings club;
- spaza shop → tuckshop, neighbourhood shop or small trader depending on scene;
- taxi rank → kombi rank or transport terminus where appropriate;
- South African township/suburb examples → Zimbabwean urban, peri-urban or rural settings selected for the story;
- community trading examples → markets, vendors, informal traders, small-scale agriculture, services and local enterprise.

Do not erase South African-origin characters or settings mechanically when the story's meaning depends on continuity. Rewrite the scene as a whole.

## 5. Character and narrative continuity

Myah and the recurring cast are part of the learning architecture.

For each recurring character:

- preserve personality, relationships and developmental arc;
- change geography, household economics or institutions only with a continuity check;
- maintain age progression across Forms;
- audit every callback after a location or economic fact changes;
- keep names unless there is a clear editorial reason to change them.

## 6. School-based projects

The Zimbabwe edition should make project work visible throughout the platform.

Each project should have:

- a real problem or observation;
- a learner plan;
- an action or test;
- evidence;
- reflection;
- a simple teacher/facilitator review point;
- a portfolio output.

Where possible, existing Applied Commerce experiments should be adapted into this structure rather than adding disconnected project tasks.

## 7. Commercial pilot standard

The first Zimbabwe offer is a one-term pilot, not a claim of curriculum replacement.

A pilot should include:

- school onboarding;
- agreed learner group;
- selected Applied Commerce stage/cycle;
- teacher or facilitator orientation;
- digital learner access;
- project/evidence capture;
- participation and completion reporting;
- end-of-pilot learner evidence summary;
- school feedback and localisation findings.

## 8. Publication gate

A Zimbabwe learner-facing unit is not publication-ready until:

- South Africa-only policy/institution references are resolved;
- all money examples make sense in Zimbabwe;
- school-stage language is correct;
- locations and community examples are credible;
- tax/legal/funding claims are verified where used;
- three-term delivery placement is assigned;
- HBC competency mapping is recorded;
- story continuity is checked;
- tests and production build pass.

## 9. Current known high-priority replacements

The five source books contain recurring references to South Africa, DBE, rand-based examples, South African locations, spaza shops, stokvels and later-stage references such as matric, NSFAS and SARS.

These are not defects in the source edition. They are the localisation workload for the Zimbabwe edition.
