# Zimbabwe Form 4 Wealth, Property & Estate Baseline — 2026

Form 4 is the final O-Level year. It contains advanced wealth, property, tax, estate, adult-money and life-launch material adapted from the South African Grade 11–12 source books.

The Zimbabwe edition must teach durable concepts while directing learners to current official rules where rates, thresholds, products or legal procedures can change.

## Primary Zimbabwe institutions

### Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA)
Use ZIMRA as the primary source for:
- income tax and PAYE;
- capital gains tax and capital-gains withholding tax;
- VAT and business tax obligations;
- tax treatment of property and marketable securities;
- current filing/payment rules.

Editorial rule: do not freeze a tax rate, allowance or threshold into learner-facing prose unless the lesson explicitly labels it as a dated illustration and tells the learner to verify the current official rule.

### Securities and Exchange Commission of Zimbabwe (SECZ)
Use SECZ as the regulator reference for:
- collective investment schemes;
- licensed securities-market participants;
- regulated investment products and investor protection.

ZSE/VFEX examples may be used where they improve understanding, but no lesson should imply that one exchange, ETF, unit trust or fund is automatically suitable for every learner.

### Insurance and Pensions Commission (IPEC)
Use IPEC as the regulator reference for:
- insurance;
- pensions;
- consumer education around regulated insurance and pension products.

### Master of the High Court / Zimbabwe estate law
Wills, deceased estates, trusts, beneficiaries and estate administration are legal topics. Teach the concepts and the importance of valid documents, named beneficiaries, record-keeping and professional advice. Do not copy South African estate-planning rules into Zimbabwe.

## Durable teaching rules

1. **Multi-currency reality**
   - Label every numeric example with its currency.
   - Do not convert rand amounts by one exchange rate.
   - Prefer simple illustrative US$ amounts where arithmetic matters.
   - Use ZiG or other currency examples only when the lesson is specifically about multi-currency decision-making.

2. **Investing**
   - Teach asset classes, diversification, fees, liquidity, risk, time horizon and rebalancing.
   - Replace South Africa-specific TFSA, RA and retail-bond assumptions with Zimbabwe-regulated product categories and official-source checking.
   - Learners should verify whether a product/provider is regulated before investing.

3. **Property**
   - Property examples must include more than purchase price and rent: vacancy, maintenance, rates/levies where applicable, financing cost, legal/transfer cost and tax.
   - Current Zimbabwe tax treatment of property can change. Teach learners to check ZIMRA before relying on a rate.
   - Do not imply property is always positive cash flow or always a superior asset.

4. **Tax**
   - Distinguish tax planning from tax evasion.
   - Use official ZIMRA guidance for current rates and obligations.
   - Capital gains tax can apply to specified assets including immovable property and marketable securities; learners must verify current rules.

5. **Estate and legacy**
   - Distinguish a will, beneficiary nomination, trust and deceased-estate administration.
   - A will does not eliminate every estate process, tax or administration requirement.
   - Do not present a trust as a universal tax-saving device.
   - Estate Duty and other estate rules must be verified against current Zimbabwe law.

6. **Final-school-year positioning**
   - Form 4 is the O-Level launch year.
   - Replace “matric” and “Grade 12” with Form 4 / O-Level language.
   - Post-school pathways must include realistic Zimbabwe options: A-Level where appropriate, tertiary/TVET pathways, employment, entrepreneurship, apprenticeships/skills development and other structured transitions.
   - Do not assume university is the default or only successful pathway.
