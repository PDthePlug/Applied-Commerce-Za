# Zimbabwe Form 3 regulatory localisation baseline

**Editorial baseline date:** 2 October 2026

This note exists to keep Applied Commerce Zimbabwe's Form 3 financial-system lessons accurate without turning a school programme into tax, investment or legal advice. Rules, rates, thresholds and product availability can change. Learner-facing examples should therefore prefer durable concepts, identify the responsible Zimbabwean institution, and clearly date any current numerical example.

## 1. Employment income and tax

- Zimbabwe Revenue Authority (ZIMRA) administers employee income tax through PAYE.
- ZIMRA publishes separate current tax tables, including USD and Zimbabwe local-currency tables.
- Do not import South African deductions or agencies such as SARS, UIF or SDL into the Zimbabwe edition.
- When a lesson needs a payslip example, distinguish gross pay, legitimate employee deductions, employer contributions, and net pay. Use an illustrative amount and state that actual deductions depend on current law and employment circumstances.
- Avoid freezing current tax brackets into evergreen learner prose. If a rate is pedagogically necessary, date it and direct learners to the latest ZIMRA table.

Official references:
- https://www.zimra.co.zw/domestic-taxes/individual/pay-as-you-earn-paye
- https://www.zimra.co.zw/domestic-taxes/tax-tables

## 2. Credit, borrowing and consumer protection

- The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe (RBZ) operates the Central Credit Registry and supervises banking-sector consumer-protection frameworks.
- Zimbabwe's credit-information system should be taught as credit records/reports and lender screening rather than importing a foreign scoring model as though it were identical.
- Debt lessons should emphasise total borrowing cost, repayment schedule, affordability, default risk, lender legitimacy, disclosure and the learner's right to understand an agreement before signing.

Official references:
- https://www.rbz.co.zw/index.php/financial-stability/credit-registry
- https://www.rbz.co.zw/index.php/public-awareness

## 3. Pensions and retirement

- The National Social Security Authority (NSSA) administers the Pension and Other Benefits Scheme for covered employees.
- The Insurance and Pensions Commission (IPEC) regulates insurance and pensions, including private occupational schemes and individual pension policies.
- Do not transplant South African retirement vehicles or tax advantages into Zimbabwe lessons without verification.
- Teach retirement as a layered system: statutory/social-security provision where applicable, employment-linked/private pension arrangements, and personal long-term saving/investing.

Official references:
- https://www.nssa.org.zw/schemes/
- https://www.nssa.org.zw/retirementbenefits/
- https://ipec.co.zw/
- https://ipec.co.zw/consumer-education/

## 4. Insurance

- IPEC is the regulator for Zimbabwe's insurance and pensions industry.
- Teach risk transfer, premiums, exclusions, excess/deductible concepts, underinsurance, claims evidence and the need to use appropriately regulated providers.
- Product names, cover limits and premium examples should be illustrative unless verified against a current policy.

Official reference:
- https://ipec.co.zw/consumer-education/

## 5. Investing and capital markets

- The Securities and Exchange Commission of Zimbabwe (SEC Zimbabwe) regulates securities-market participants.
- Zimbabwe learners can be taught shares and collective investment schemes as regulated capital-market concepts; provider and product availability must be checked before naming a live option.
- Do not present South African-specific products such as retail savings bonds, JSE-only examples or South African TFSA rules as Zimbabwe products.
- Teach diversification, risk, fees, liquidity, time horizon, licensed intermediaries and the difference between saving and investing before product selection.

Official references:
- https://seczim.co.zw/faqs/
- https://seczim.co.zw/collective-investment-schemes/
- https://seczim.co.zw/licensing-requirements/securities-dealing-firms-and-securities-dealers/

## 6. Wills and deceased estates

- Zimbabwe has a Wills Act [Chapter 6:06] and Administration of Estates Act [Chapter 6:01].
- The Master/Assistant Master of the High Court has statutory roles in the administration and registration processes described by the legislation.
- Learner material should teach the purpose of a will, heirs/beneficiaries, an executor, record-keeping and the value of obtaining qualified legal advice. It should not give a learner a DIY legal instrument and call it legally sufficient.
- Any discussion of will formalities should be treated as a current-law snapshot and checked against the latest legislation before publication.

Legal references:
- https://zimlii.org/akn/zw/act/1987/13/
- https://zimlii.org/akn/zw/act/ord/1907/6/

## Editorial rule

For every regulated Form 3 lesson ask:

1. Is this a durable concept or a current rule?
2. If it is a current rule, which Zimbabwe institution owns it?
3. Does the lesson need the current number, or can the concept be taught without freezing a rate?
4. If a numerical example is used, is it labelled **illustrative** or **current as at [date]**?
5. Does the lesson clearly distinguish education from personalised financial, tax or legal advice?
