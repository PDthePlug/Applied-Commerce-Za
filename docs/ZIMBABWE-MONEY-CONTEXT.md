# Zimbabwe Money Context Standard — 2026

Applied Commerce Zimbabwe must treat currency as part of the learning context, not as a find-and-replace exercise.

## Current policy baseline

As of 2026, Zimbabwe operates a **multi-currency system**. The Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe's 2026 Foreign Exchange Transactions Guidelines state that foreign currencies may be used as legal tender alongside the local currency, Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG). The Reserve Bank has also stated that movement toward exclusive local-currency use is gradual and conditional rather than a simple date-based switch.

Primary references:

- Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe, *Foreign Exchange Transactions Guidelines FXD5/2026*: https://www.rbz.co.zw/documents/Regulations_Acts/2026/Foreign_Exchange_Guidelines_FXD5_version_2._March_2026.pdf
- Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe, *2026 Monetary Policy Statement*: https://www.rbz.co.zw/documents/mps/2026/MPS_FINAL_-_FULL.pdf

## Editorial rules

1. Never convert every South African rand amount using one exchange rate.
2. Every numeric money example must identify its currency explicitly: e.g. **ZiG** or **US$**.
3. Do not mix currencies inside one calculation unless exchange rates or multi-currency decision-making are the lesson.
4. Prefer durable examples over quickly dated retail prices.
5. Where a lesson teaches a concept rather than a price, use a currency-neutral goal or ask the learner to insert a current local amount.
6. When real price examples matter, verify them close to publication and record the date/context.
7. Later lessons should use Zimbabwe's multi-currency reality as a learning opportunity: budgeting, pricing, saving, exchange-rate risk and purchasing power can all be taught more authentically because learners live inside that system.
8. ZiG should not be treated as a renamed rand. USD should not be treated as the only Zimbabwean currency.
9. Banking, interest, tax and formal-finance examples require current Zimbabwe verification before publication.
10. Teacher/facilitator guidance should permit a current local example to replace an illustrative amount without changing the underlying concept.

## Form 1 approach

For the first Form 1 localisation pass:

- remove rand-only assumptions;
- use currency-neutral examples where the number is not pedagogically important;
- introduce explicitly labelled ZiG or US$ examples only where a numeric amount improves the lesson;
- preserve a later dedicated opportunity to teach why the same learner may encounter more than one currency in everyday economic life.
